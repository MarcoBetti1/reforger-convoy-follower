## Current convoy redesign and player test

The current source forms one convoy per player, with a hard cap of five trucks. **Unit One follows the player's vehicle; Unit Two follows Unit One's truck; each later unit follows the truck immediately ahead.** Only the convoy leader speaks to the ordering player. The session routes the leader's own events to short "we/us" calls and relays later units' range, stuck, lost, and rejoined events as numbered Unit N calls. Routine ready, following, and holding updates from later units are silent. The first chain build was observed following and its processed voice calls were audible; the latest polish changes need a new player test.

### Destination unloading and return (new behavior to test)

When the player's lead vehicle stops for several seconds, followers approach it in order and stay seated; exiting the vehicle also starts this arrival behavior. No cargo is loaded or unloaded by the addon. At the stopping spot, the owner unloads the front **M923A1** manually, then uses **Pull off and regroup** from outside at that truck's existing rear cargo interaction menu. This is the only release order. The driver is meant to turn to a clear road slot behind the line and park facing the way the convoy came. Only after that truck has physically reached its return slot does the next truck approach the same unloading spot. Repeat for each truck being unloaded. A failed or blocked turn should hold the line rather than advance it.

Returned trucks form a parked return line and do not follow the player just because the player enters a vehicle. The intended return event occurs when the owner drives the lead vehicle homeward past the parked line. It activates returned trucks and has any still-waiting outbound trucks turn once to join them, then the combined convoy follows the player's vehicle back. If the player continues outbound instead, the unreleased trucks resume ordinary following while the parked return line waits. There is no autonomous route to a stored base and no separate return action. This route and the rear-menu interaction have passed component-level headless work but **have not been verified in live gameplay**; use the five-truck test below before relying on them in a scenario.

1. In the open World Editor, press `F5` to preview and enter as a US player at the base apron. The saved scene has three Convoy Driver groups and four trucks. Cargo loading remains manual.
2. To stage one or more drivers, use **Follow me on foot** while you are outside vehicles. The new build uses an entity FOLLOW waypoint per driver. When you enter a car, truck, or helicopter, each staged driver attempts a free cargo seat in that same vehicle, never the controls; on exit, they try to dismount and resume following on foot. If no seat is free or boarding times out after 25 seconds, they hold outside. **Stop following on foot** cancels a staged driver's order; only the player who gave it can issue the stop. Multiple walkers and passenger transport need a live test.
3. At the truck, use **Start convoy in closest empty vehicle** on that staged driver, or use it immediately on an idle driver already beside a truck. The driver selects the nearest empty wheeled vehicle within 35 m, including a Jeep; check which one is closest. Wait until it takes the driver's seat. This NPC is Unit One. Stay on foot to add more drivers, then enter your own vehicle to start moving.
4. While on foot, visit the second preplaced driver. Either use **Add to convoy in closest empty vehicle** there, or use **Follow me on foot** to bring it to the desired vehicle first. Once it boards, Unit Two should track Unit One's truck rather than the player's vehicle. Add the third preplaced driver the same way to test Unit Three tracking Unit Two. Enter the remaining truck yourself after all orders are placed.
5. To test replacement, leave one preplaced driver idle or spawn another one-member Convoy Driver group and an empty wheeled vehicle. You may stage the candidate on foot first. **Replace convoy leader with this driver** boards the candidate before changing the convoy. Once the correct driver seat is confirmed, the old leader leaves the convoy and dismounts. The remaining units stay in order behind the new leader. If boarding fails, the old leader remains in charge.
6. **Stand down** removes a seated driver from this player's convoy; later units reconnect to the preceding unit. Stopping or getting out of the player's vehicle should bring the trucks closer while the drivers stay seated. Reentering and driving away should resume ordinary following if no unloading maneuver is underway.
7. Observe the actual order and spacing of the vehicles, which vehicle each NPC selected, whether anyone stops or gets stuck, and which spoken calls are heard. The leader should warn at 180 m sustained separation from the player, then become lost after 10 seconds beyond 275 m. Later units use those thresholds relative to their immediate predecessor. Check `[ConvoyFollower]` log entries alongside what happened on screen; the log alone does not prove movement or audio playback.

For a supply handoff test, add two more one-member driver groups and two M923A1 transport trucks to the prepared scene, then order all five drivers. Drive far enough to establish an approach route, stop in a clear area, and inspect the rear cargo menu of the front assigned truck. Load and unload supplies yourself. After unloading each truck, choose **Pull off and regroup** and observe the truck's actual turn, parked position, and the next truck's arrival at the same bay. After releasing some or all trucks, drive the player vehicle homeward past the parked return line and check whether returned units and any unreleased outbound units form one convoy without a repeated turn. Also test driving onward instead: only unreleased trucks should resume following. If the menu order fails or a turn is blocked, record the vehicle positions and `[ConvoyFollower]` log; a waypoint or log message alone does not prove a safe maneuver. The rear order is installed on M923A1 and standard Ural4320 transport cargo parts; the latter still needs a live menu and road smoke test. Covered Ural inheritance and other vehicles remain outside the verified interaction scope.

## Latest live pass and combat retest

In the polish preview, the log recorded one staged driver boarding a cargo seat, riding, and beginning to exit after the player exited. It also recorded `STOP_SETTLE` for all three convoy trucks, but their final visual spacing remains unverified. The player reported that all three drivers left their trucks as soon as hostile fire began; the log showed `ABORTED` for each, with no preceding mod-issued stand-down, vehicle-destroyed, or terminal-stall event. The game AI's exact internal trigger was not logged. The old under-fire monitor also logged that the driver damage manager was unavailable, so that preview could not verify the hit report.

The current build gives active convoy driving waypoints Game Master priority, above the documented autonomous get-out priority. An unexpected exit by a live driver with a viable assigned truck now preserves that unit's roster place while it tries the assigned driver seat up to three times, 12 seconds apart. Successful reboarding resumes the chain; a dead driver, destroyed truck, wrong seat, or exhausted attempts removes the unit. Explicit **Stand down** still removes it immediately. The damage monitor now retries late attachment; the reopened Workbench scene logged successful attachment to all three drivers. Truck attachment and an actual hit-triggered radio call still need a live check. At a stop or while the player is on foot, the move waypoint can finish or be cleared, so forced priority alone may not prevent an AI combat exit.

The session now ends after a short grace period if the ordering player disconnects or the original character is confirmed dead. It issues stand-down to every active and parked driver, then clears the old convoy so a new one can be ordered. Temporarily controlling another entity in Game Master pauses lead-vehicle detection without rebinding or ending the session. The code passed Workbench script validation, but still needs a live death/respawn check; a blocked get-out waypoint can leave a driver seated after the session ends.

The user visually confirmed a single follower driving behind the lead vehicle in a truck. A driver in the Jeep also physically followed in a later run. In an older test, the player exited the lead vehicle to order a second driver, which made the first follower automatically stand down and dismount. In the latest visual test of the earlier independent-follower version, the player stayed on foot while ordering two Convoy Drivers, then entered a lead vehicle. Both trucks visibly followed at once. When the player drove beyond the old range, both stopped, but there was no spoken status in that build. The next radio build added private audible calls for **ready**, **holding**, **following**, **range warning**, **stuck**, **lost**, and **rejoined** and passed validation and packaging. Playback and the new distance behavior still need a fresh in-game test. A real in-game radio item or frequency is not required. The current source also monitors confirmed kinetic, fragmentation, or explosive damage to an active driver or assigned truck and emits one **under fire** call per attack episode, rearming after 18 seconds without another qualifying hit. That trigger needs a live test. No HUD message or separate UI is used.

The player's first two WAV recordings were processed into radio-style clips under `Sounds/Radio/`, with exact boundaries and checksums in [`docs/convoy-radio-assets.json`](../../docs/convoy-radio-assets.json) and a reproducible processing script at [`tools/prepare_convoy_radio.py`](../../tools/prepare_convoy_radio.py). Those first-person clips belong to the earlier independent-driver prototype. The player's new `unit.wav` leader recording was cut into 58 stronger radio-style calls under `Sounds/Leader/`, with resource IDs, durations, and checksums in [`docs/convoy-leader-assets.json`](../../docs/convoy-leader-assets.json). The session routes active calls through the ordering player's controller for private local playback, without radio equipment or distance limits. Under-fire status uses confirmed damage rather than a guessed nearby-threat state. Audible playback and this new trigger still need an in-game check.

There is no driver-facing voice toggle. Addon authors can set `m_bVoiceEnabled` in `Configs/CF_ConvoySettings.conf` to silence or enable private calls for every player in a rebuilt addon. The same config controls routine call chance, routine spacing, repeated routine phrases, and repeated range/rejoined status. Stuck, lost, and under-fire reports are not dropped by the new debounce; incoming exceptions remove stale queued routine chatter before playback. These timing changes passed headless script validation and still need an in-game audio pass.

## Asset structure

Game Master has US and Soviet **Convoy Driver** groups. The US group contains a crew AI character with an M9; the Soviet group uses Soviet crew gear and a PM. Spawn a group card, not a character prefab directly: the behavior uses the driver's parent `SCR_AIGroup` to assign boarding and follow waypoints. The Soviet card and interaction have passed Workbench validation and packing, but still need a live smoke test.

The character prefab inherits the game's `Character_US_Crew.et`, including its replicated character setup, and adds `CF_DriverControllerComponent` and `CF_UnderFireMonitorComponent`. It clears the crew carbine slot, equips an M9 in the secondary slot, and replaces the rifle magazines with three M9 magazines. It appends `CF_FollowOnFootAction`, `CF_StopOnFootAction`, `CF_AssignTruckAction` (Start), `CF_AddConvoyAction`, `CF_ReplaceLeaderAction`, and `CF_StandDownAction` to the inherited `ActionsManagerComponent` under the existing `default` interaction context. The component IDs were inspected from the installed base game's `data007.pak` (version 1.8.0.13), not guessed.

The Soviet character inherits `Character_USSR_Crew.et`, clears its inherited primary weapon, equips a PM and three magazines, and adds the same convoy controller, damage monitor, and interactions. Its one-member Soviet group is registered in the same placeable group catalog as the US group.

The unload order is a separate additive override of the vanilla `M923A1_cargo.et` part. It appends `CF_ReleaseAtUnloadAction` to that part's existing `door_rear` action context, alongside vanilla supply load/unload and cargo storage actions. The M923A1 transport slot registers the part's actions with the truck. Its visibility is owner-checked and limited to the seated front convoy driver after arrival; no truck door or extra handle is required. The matching prefab override passed a headless pack check, but the rear scroll menu still needs a player-facing check.

The same action is appended to the vanilla Ural4320 cargo part's existing `door_rear` context. The Soviet prefab and Ural override were checked against the installed base-game pack, then validated and packed headlessly. The Ural rear menu still needs a player-facing check.

The group is added to Game Master through an explicit placeable group registry referenced from the additive `EditorModeEdit.et` override. This follows Bohemia's sample registration path, but still needs an in-game spawn check; the prepared scene's preplaced driver does not verify the card. The standalone character is deliberately absent from the registry, because direct placement may leave it without a parent AI group.

Primary source patterns: Bohemia's `SampleMod_NewFaction` group prefab, placeable registry and editor override; Bohemia's Game Master editable entities documentation; the installed game's `Character_Base.et` component IDs and action context. No vanilla files were changed.
