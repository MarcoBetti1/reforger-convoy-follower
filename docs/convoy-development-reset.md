## Product we are building

A player recruits drivers, loads supplies, leads a convoy along a sensible drivable route, stops to unload, and resumes with the same assignments. Unit One follows the player. Every other unit follows its predecessor. The leader alone communicates useful status. Hold in the vehicle, dismount, and dismiss remain distinct operations.

The route is chosen by the player. Reliable movement is the foundation for the complete recruitment, assignment, travel, disruption, arrival, unloading, regrouping, and return experience. Start with ordinary drivable routes, then improve the whole workflow and its controls, communication, multiplayer behavior, configuration, performance, and distribution. A first successful supply trip is an intermediate milestone. Unexpected obstructions should produce an honest hold/problem state.

The hard cap is **five recruited follower vehicles, excluding the player's lead vehicle**. Focus physical development on one, two, and three followers. Preserve the daisy chain and one prerecorded radio spokesperson while allowing evidence-led architectural change. Work primarily in scripts, configuration, UI, inherited assets, and necessary fixtures. Helicopters, new model/animation pipelines, general combat command, and an autonomous logistics economy remain outside the central assignment.

## What slowed the recent work

- Arrival, forward parking, passing the parked line, and return merging were tested as a long sequence. Early setup failures often prevented the intended behavior from running.
- Some short tests introduced a new scripted owner pilot. Its displayed throttle did not survive to the physics step. A previous unsigned-distance gate even counted downhill coasting. That test-driver fault was separate from follower navigation.
- A field fixture was selected near vegetation and a wall; its own clearance survey rejected it before any driving occurred.
- Historical handoff documents continued to describe old builds as current. The entry instructions now point to the concise validation checkpoint.
- The existing controller already records predecessor samples, but route progress, road projection, AI waypoint lifecycle, parking, and radio are tightly mixed. More conditionals in that component make comparisons harder.

## Experiment and evidence discipline

Before a controlled run, record the question, hypothesis, frozen pack hash, effective settings, physical support/reject criteria, observation window, and next decision. A provisional PASS marker does not end observation. Use at least the sustained-hold window above and extend it when the failure mechanism requires it. Keep full logs, terminal snapshots, post-action video, and separate gameplay/shutdown results. Preserve unresolved runtime errors; only the exact Helipad error currently has an independently reproduced vanilla baseline.

If a branch never runs, diagnose fixture or ownership before tuning follower behavior. When repeated experiments stop producing information, change the hypothesis, observations, abstraction, or method rather than imposing a one-repair limit. Separate fixture faults, convoy faults, environment limitations, and misleading measurements. Exploratory playtests should become repeatable cases for consequential failures. Static geometry, compilation, command acceptance, physical execution, human UI input, multiplayer, and cleanup establish different things.

The historically named Arland clear-field world is an off-network **concrete taxiway**; use `--fixture arland --surface off-network`. Dirt/grass claims require moving natural-material wheel evidence from the relevant vehicles. Road-graph distance alone is insufficient. Label changed terrain/navigation as a changed fixture. Use fresh-launch repeats, varied routes, and sustained observation where they resolve a concrete risk.
