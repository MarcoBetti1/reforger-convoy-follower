Revised project goal
Build an excellent, release-quality NPC convoy and logistics mod for Arma Reforger: something players choose because it makes transporting supplies enjoyable, dependable, and easy to control. Develop the entire experience, from recruiting and assigning drivers through coordinated travel, arrival, unloading, regrouping, and return. Make the convoy handle ordinary disruptions intelligently, communicate clearly, and behave predictably in multiplayer.
Preserve the deliberate player → squad leader → predecessor-following chain, one radio spokesperson, the existing five-vehicle cap, and the emphasis on scripting and existing assets. Give the implementation room to evolve substantially. Research difficult engine behavior, compare competing designs, build effective development tools, and refactor or replace inadequate components when evidence supports it.
Treat reliable basic following as the foundation for a polished product. Continue through worthwhile improvements in control, recovery, logistics, usability, performance, and maintainability. Keep useful progress integrated, versioned, and demonstrated in the actual game. The intended result is a complete, well-supported release candidate with convincing evidence for its capabilities and clearly understood limitations.
What this version changes
There is no artificial overnight deadline, diagnostic-attempt limit, or credit budget. Difficult problems deserve sustained investigation. Completing the first supply run is an intermediate milestone. More sophisticated arrival behavior, individual control, recovery, and return journeys belong in the development program.
The work should remain purposeful. An experiment should improve understanding; a tool should improve development or verification; a feature should improve the experience. Research depth, extensive testing, and major engineering work are welcome when they serve those purposes. Checkpoints preserve progress throughout the assignment; they do not define its ambition.

2. Own the product vision and preserve its identity.
The player leads a convoy. Unit One follows the player's vehicle, and each subsequent driver follows its predecessor. The leader serves as spokesperson for the convoy using prerecorded audio. Preserve that intent while improving route sharing, coordination, ordering, and recovery. Internal information sharing is welcome; having every truck independently chase the player's current position is not an acceptable substitute.
Retain the existing hard cap of five and focus physical driving development on one, two, and three followers, as previously requested. State clearly how vehicle counts are defined. Greater ambition means better behavior, completeness, and polish within this design.
Keep the work predominantly in scripting, configuration, UI, and inherited game assets. Make necessary prefab wiring and test-world improvements. Evaluate useful dependencies on their technical merit, compatibility, redistribution terms, and installation burden. New models, animation pipelines, helicopters, general combat command, and an entire autonomous logistics economy are outside this assignment's central purpose.
The desired experience is a player who recruits drivers, assigns vehicles, loads supplies, travels, handles a disruption, reaches the destination, unloads, reorganizes, and returns without losing control of the convoy. Build toward that complete experience and look for opportunities to make it satisfying.

- A complete supply trip through normal player controls.
- Robust operation with multiple followers and realistic interruptions.
- Complete assignment, individual command, arrival, regrouping, and return workflows.

These are a starting sequence. Reorder work when dependencies or evidence justify it. A basic successful trip should lead into the next worthwhile improvement. Preserve working milestones while developing more advanced behavior.
Identify the most consequential weakness in the actual experience, form a hypothesis, make or prototype an improvement, validate it, integrate it, and reassess. Do not stop because a problem has taken a long time or because two attempts failed. When experiments cease to produce useful information, change the explanation, observations, abstraction, or method. Persist intelligently.
If a task is blocked, investigate the blocker, advance independent useful work, and return when a better route is available. External blockers must be concrete and supported by evidence. Difficulty alone is not a reason to declare a capability impossible.
4. Understand and improve the driving system at the right level.
Investigate the interaction between convoy coordination, predecessor route history, native navigation, waypoint lifecycle, speed control, stopping, and recovery. Avoid treating every symptom as a single generic pathfinding problem.
Resolve movement ownership: which native behavior currently controls the driver, which system issues or replaces requests, what makes an activity complete, and what can unexpectedly recreate movement. Use the installed engine scripts and official documentation. Public API signatures are leads to investigate, not proof that a particular call sequence works.
Compare viable strategies in controlled fixtures. The existing driven-route helper and native cruise experiment are promising starting points. You may improve or replace components if a comparative prototype demonstrates a better foundation. Preserve native steering and obstacle handling where they work; any deeper intervention needs clear ownership and evidence that it improves physical behavior.
Aim for smooth, predictable driving: appropriate following distance at different speeds, sensible braking, prompt startup, clean turns, stable ordering, and progress without repeated waypoint resets. Consider vehicle dimensions, actual closing speed, route curvature, route discontinuities, and the difference between intentional waiting and genuine obstruction. Verify distance conventions and speed units.
Route tracking should preserve progress through turns and hairpins, avoid skipping forward onto a nearby later segment, and handle changed targets or missing history deliberately. A point on a recorded trail still needs to be usable by the actual vehicle and its navigation system.
Coordinate recovery across the convoy. A truck waiting behind a held or blocked predecessor should not independently start a conflicting maneuver. Recovery should have understandable stages, remain interruptible by a player command, and eventually provide a useful explanation or request for intervention if progress is impossible. Do not hide unresolved behavior in endless retries.
Handle vehicle loss, driver death, missed turns, temporary obstruction, changed order, and player exit/reboarding without corrupting the session. A local failure should have a deliberate downstream outcome. Avoid automatic recovery that surprises the player, moves a held convoy without permission, or interferes with a player-occupied vehicle.

6. Make the complete logistics workflow excellent.
Develop preparation, travel, arrival, unloading, regrouping, and return as one coherent experience.
Make recruitment and vehicle assignment explicit and discoverable. Preserve reservations during boarding. Support correcting a mistaken assignment, replacing an unavailable driver, changing convoy order, and changing the spokesperson without needlessly destroying membership or vehicle assignments. Handle conflicts and invalid choices with useful feedback.
Complete whole-convoy and individual control where it helps players: follow/resume, hold, temporary dismount, reboard, dismiss, reassignment, and sensible spacing control. Keep their meanings distinct. Dismount should preserve the relationship needed to resume; dismissal should clearly release it. Reconnect the chain deliberately when a member leaves or changes position.
Treat arrival and departure as real gameplay features. Investigate and finish explicit pull-off, regroup, moving a truck clear of the unloading point, advancing the next truck, and rejoining for the return journey. The existing forward-wait and rear-return experiments are useful starting points. Determine which maneuvers are reliable under which conditions, improve them, and choose defaults that players can understand.
Validate space, approach, clearance, and the ability to depart. If there is no suitable parking or turning area, retain a safe predictable state and explain the limitation. A maneuver should not claim completion while a truck is still blocking the next one. Do not rely on teleportation or invisible resets for normal convoy operation.
Use the game's existing player-controlled supply actions as the dependable loading and unloading baseline. You may investigate optional player-commanded assistance where native systems support it and it improves usability; keep transfers explicit and under the player's authority. Prove actual transfer at the destination. Consider useful additions such as cargo visibility, destination markers, staging controls, and delivery progress when the installed systems expose the necessary information. Keep new capabilities clearly distinguished from engine features you have actually verified.
Explore optional route or destination assistance after the core loop is solid if it meaningfully improves this player-led experience. Preserve the player's authority over the trip. The objective is an excellent convoy mod with a clear purpose.
